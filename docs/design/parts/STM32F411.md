# STM32F411RET6 — layout constraints

Record: COL-COTS-0024 · Datasheet: `Refs/Datasheets/STM32F411xC-xE.pdf`

The alternate-function and ADC-channel claims below are read out of that PDF's
Table 9 (alternate function mapping) and its pin-definition table. The decoupling
scheme is distilled from ST's power-supply scheme for the family, recorded in the
COTS record.

## Role on this board

Sole controller. Five motor channels (EN=PWM, PH=GPIO per axis) and five current-sense
ADC inputs. See `boards/MC3_COL_MAIN_V1.0/MCU.tsx`.

## Placement constraints

**Bypass adjacency — the binding constraint on this part.**

Two different numbers, and the difference is currently the problem. *Placed* is the
pad-to-pad distance, i.e. how good the placement is. *Routed* is the length of copper the
autorouter actually laid down, which is what the inductance follows. Both measured from
the built Circuit JSON.

| Cap | Value | Pin | Max | Placed | Routed | Movable? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `C_MCU_VDD1` | 100nF | VDD1 | 3mm | 1.39mm | 4.84mm (GND leg) ⚠ | **No** |
| `C_MCU_VDD2` | 100nF | VDD2 | 3mm | 1.39mm | 5.57mm (GND leg) ⚠ | **No** |
| `C_MCU_VDD3` | 100nF | VDD3 | 3mm | 1.98mm | 6.13mm ⚠ | **No** |
| `C_MCU_VDD4` | 100nF | VDD4 | 3mm | 2.41mm | within budget | **No** |
| `C_MCU_VDDA_HF` | 100nF | VDDA | 3mm | 1.88mm | 11.84mm ⚠ | **No** |
| `C_MCU_VCAP` | 4.7µF | VCAP_1 | 3mm | 1.87mm | within budget | **No** |
| `C_MCU_VDDA` | 1µF | VDDA | 6mm | 3.56mm | 6.98mm ⚠ | Yes |
| `C_MCU_VBAT` | 100nF | VBAT | 9mm | 7.18mm | 13.91mm ⚠ | Yes, freely |
| `C_MCU_BULK` | 4.7µF | 3.3V rail | — | unconstrained | — | Yes, anywhere |

⚠ **The placement is good and the routing throws it away.** `maxDecouplingTraceLength` is
declared on every one of these and the autorouter treats it as advisory — it emits
`pcb_trace_too_long_warning` and routes anyway. The cause is that these caps connect to
*nets* (`net.V3_3`, `net.GND`), not to specific pins, so the router is free to satisfy
connectivity at any point on the net: a cap sitting 1.9mm from VDDA can be routed 11.8mm
around to reach V3_3 somewhere else entirely. Elsewhere on this board it is far worse —
`C_leaf4_VM` is ~1mm from its driver's VM pin and routed **24.4mm**.

Until this is addressed, **the decoupling on this board is not delivered as designed** and
the `V = L·di/dt` reasoning below describes the intent rather than the artifact. The fix
is to route cap-to-pin point-to-point — as the Kelvin sense pairs in `MotorChannel.tsx`
already do, connecting to `shunt.pin1`/`pin2` rather than to the nets those pads sit on —
and to let only the return leg reach the plane. #tbd

One 100nF **per VDD pin** — they are not interchangeable, because each pin has its own
bond wire and the path between them runs through the die. Rationale and the
`V = L·di/dt` arithmetic are in the Main-Board-01 build rung; the short version is that ~1 nH/mm of
loop inductance makes 2mm vs 10mm a ~5× difference in supply droop.

`C_MCU_VCAP` is **not** a decoupling cap — it compensates the internal 1.2V core LDO.
Low-ESR X5R/X7R, and it is not optional: without it the core regulator does not start
reliably. Do not shrink it to reclaim area.

**Consequence for the cluster envelope.** VDD1–4 sit on four *different* package edges,
so their caps expand the cluster in all four directions. That is inherent to LQFP64 and
cannot be laid out away. The three caps that tolerate distance (`C_MCU_VDDA`,
`C_MCU_VBAT`, `C_MCU_BULK`) are already parked in space the cluster occupies anyway —
adding all three cost 0mm of width and 0.6mm of height.

**Other placement:**
- `XT1` crystal directly below the OSC pins (pin5/pin6, bottom edge at x≈−1.5) to keep
  the oscillator loop short; load caps flank it at ±3.1mm.
- `C_MCU_VCAP` is staggered 1mm along the edge and 0.5mm further out than its
  neighbours: VCAP_1 and VDD2 are only 1mm apart on the same edge, and the 0603
  courtyard (2.45mm) clips the chip pads at the shared radius.
- `C_MCU_VDDA_HF` sits 1mm further out than the other HF caps — at the shared radius the
  router had to thread V3_3 between the chip edge and PC13, producing a pad-to-trace
  violation and a via inside PC13's pad.
- `J_SWD` stacked above the chip. `pinrow4` is **already horizontal at rotation 0**;
  adding `pcbRotation={90}` turns it into a 9.12mm vertical column.

## Pin assignment is a layout constraint

**Which pin a signal gets decides which side of the package it escapes from, and the
router cannot fix a bad choice.** This is the single highest-leverage layout constraint
on this part — higher than anything in the placement section.

The MCU sits at (−21, 2) on a 64mm disc, so **every one of the five channels is east of
it**. That fixes what each package edge is good for:

| Edge | Faces | Use for |
|---|---|---|
| E | all four leaves | anything; the safe default |
| N | leaf1 / leaf2 drivers (y = +25.2) | their EN, PH |
| S | leaf3 / leaf4 drivers (y = −25.2) | their EN, PH, ISENSE |
| W | the board rim | **nothing** — leave empty |

Two silicon limits shape what is reachable:

- **There are no ADC pins on the N edge.** ADC1 is PA0–PA7, PB0, PB1, PC0–PC5. The N edge
  carries only PA8–PA13, PC6–PC9, PB12–PB15. So a current-sense input can never exit
  north, and `ISENSE_leaf1` turns a corner to reach its amplifier. Not fixable in layout.
- **TIM3 is the only 4-channel timer reaching both N and E.** Each channel has two pin
  options — CH1 {PA6|PC6}, CH2 {PA7|PC7}, CH3 {PB0|PC8}, CH4 {PB1|PC9} — and mixing them
  per channel is legal. That is what lets all four EN PWMs share one timer *and* still
  exit on the correct side. TIM4 (PB6–PB9) is W-edge only: never use it here.

**Why it matters, concretely.** An earlier revision assigned pins in numeric order
(EN=PA0–3, PH=PB0–2/PB10, ISENSE=PC0–3, ENC=PB6–9). 12 of 16 leaf signals then left the
package pointing away from their load, the router dived to an inner layer at the pad to
cross back under the die, and one via landed *inside* `U_MCU.PA0`'s own SMD pad. At
0.5mm pitch with 0.3mm pads the gaps are 0.2mm, so a 0.3mm via has nowhere legal to go —
the DRC error was unavoidable downstream of the pin choice. Reassigning by edge took it
to 13/16 and the error cleared with no other change.

The three that still turn a corner are constrained, not sloppy: `ISENSE_leaf1` (no N-edge
ADC), `EN_leaf3` (TIM3 has no S-edge mapping), `ENC_leaf2` (its connector sits near the
N/E diagonal).

**If you re-pin this part, re-check the edge map before the netlist.** The live
assignment and its per-pin reasoning are in `boards/MC3_COL_MAIN_V1.0/MCU.tsx`.

Also: **PB2 is BOOT1.** Do not hang a driver signal on it.

## Routing constraints

- Decoupling returns (`decouplingTo`) assume a GND pour. Several budgets above are sized
  for the *supply* leg only; the ground leg is a via to plane, not a trace.
- **That assumption is currently not fully met.** `<copperpour>` fills leftover space but
  does not reserve a layer from the autorouter, and there is no config option that does.
  On the current build the router runs ~143 trace segments through `inner1` (the ground
  pour) and ~168 through `inner2`. The plane under any given part has to be checked
  rather than assumed. Unresolved — see the stackup comment in
  `boards/MC3_COL_MAIN_V1.0/MC3_COL_MAIN_V1.0.tsx`.

## Gotchas that affect layout

- **Pin labels cannot contain hyphens.** `PC14-OSC32_IN` was silently dropped from the
  component until renamed `PC14_OSC32_IN` in `imports/STM32F411RET6.tsx`.
- Cluster is ~17.7 × 24.3mm. The annulus is 17.5mm wide on-axis and 14.55mm on a
  diagonal, so **this does not fit yet** — see COL-COTS-0024's reopened package-fit note.
